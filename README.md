# R code for Proteomic-profiling-of-in-vitro-cultured-neonatal-and-adult-megakaryopoiesis_ manuscript
# Abstract
Megakaryopoiesis, the differentiation of hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) into megakaryocytes (MKs) is highly regulated process involving extensive proteomic remodelling. Although, age-related development has been shown to affect both megakaryocytes and platelets in respect to their development, the proteomic mechanisms underlying these differences remain incompletely understood. In this study, we employed mass spectrometry to systematically profile the proteomes of in vitro cultured MKs derived from neonatal (cord blood- CB) and adult (mobilized peripheral blood- MPB) sources. By mapping proteomic shifts across time, we identified divergent patterns specific to each source. Established megakaryopoiesis essential proteins (e.g. TUBB1, TBXAS, MPIG6B, GP9, GP1BB and GP1BA) emerged later in neonatal megakaryocytes. Platelet-important proteins are found consistently lower in CB-derived MKs (e.g. F2RL3, ADRA2A, PPBP, PF4 and ITGB3). Using differential protein levels to infer transcription factor activity in silico, we identified CREB1, JUND, MAFG, BCL11AA, AHCTF1, ARID3A, HMGA2 and YY1, as potential novel regulators of age-associated megakaryopoiesis. Comparative analysis between our MK cultures and platelets from previous studies revealed discrepancies that transcended from MKs to platelets such as VWF, IGF2BP3 and MARCKS. These findings provide insights into the proteomic dynamics shaping neonatal and adult megakaryopoiesis, contextualizing the functional age-related disparities in MK and platelet biology.
